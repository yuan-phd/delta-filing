# Abstract

Length-normalized DPO with an SFT retention term drives the implicit reward margin to ~7 β-scaled units in a 4B+LoRA model trained on structured exact-format tasks. The dominant framing — displacement theory, IPO's design motivation — predicts this should degrade capability uniformly through chosen-likelihood decline. In a pre-registered 8-seed study with artifact-aware scoring, we find the alignment tax is real but graded: ranging from undetectable (extraction, Δ = −0.5, TOST equivalence PASS) through small but statistically genuine (json, Δ = −3.5, one-directional loss, TOST boundary FAIL) to catastrophic (router, Δ = −30), tracking format-alignment to the preference data more than margin magnitude. The retention term anchors chosen-completion likelihood while DPO gradients suppress rejected completions; the entire margin is rejected collapse, channeled predominantly through the preference data's style axis. Gold-logp deflation is task-selective (extraction −0.15, json −1.04, router −1.47 nats/token, fp16), directionally consistent with behavioral outcomes, with gold selectively protected relative to incorrect controls on extraction (8/8 seeds). Artifact-aware scoring and pre-registered TOST equivalence testing prevented a false-collapse conclusion from a naive scorer. Scope: LN rewards with retention, LoRA, 4B model, easily-separable synthetic preferences.
