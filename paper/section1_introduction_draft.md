# §1 Introduction

DPO's implicit reward margin grows large under length-normalized rewards — to ~7 β-scaled units in our experiments — echoing the unbounded growth IPO was designed to prevent in the standard formulation. The dominant framing in the literature treats this as pathological: Razin et al. (2025) identify likelihood displacement, where chosen log-prob decreases and probability mass may flow to out-of-distribution completions; IPO (Azar et al., 2024) was explicitly designed to bound the margin, treating unbounded growth as the core problem; and Cal-DPO (Xiao et al., 2024) observes absolute likelihood deflation and proposes a calibration fix. Yet the empirical basis for these concerns comes primarily from judge-based evaluation or full fine-tuning at large scale. The common practical regime — small model, LoRA, length-normalized rewards, structured exact-format tasks — is understudied. And a methodological gap compounds the empirical one: the literature lacks equivalence testing for capability preservation. Studies sometimes report "no significant difference" without the statistical power to distinguish "preserved" from "underpowered to detect a real drop."

This paper presents a pre-registered 8-seed study measuring behavioral capability (free-generation with artifact-aware scoring) and probability-layer dynamics (per-token gold/control log-probabilities) as the implicit reward margin grows to ~7 β-scaled units in a 4B model fine-tuned with LoRA. Our training objective combines length-normalized DPO with an SFT retention term (weight 0.5) that anchors chosen-completion likelihood — a common practical choice that, as we show, channels DPO's optimization pressure entirely into rejected-completion suppression.

The alignment tax is real but graded: it ranges from undetectable on one task (extraction, Δ = −0.5, TOST equivalence PASS) through small but statistically genuine on a second (json, Δ = −3.5, one-directional loss) to catastrophic on a third (router, Δ = −30). The gradient tracks format-alignment to the preference data's dominant style axis, consistently across all 8 seeds. With three tasks, we report this co-occurrence without claiming causation.

The mechanism is asymmetric: the retention term keeps chosen reward approximately stationary while DPO gradients drive rejected reward to −14.2, producing margin growth entirely through rejected-completion collapse. The style axis dominates this suppression through volume (2.2× more pairs), not per-pair potency — a distinction confirmed when the pre-registered prediction of style per-pair dominance was falsified on held-out data. Gold-logp deflation is itself task-selective and directionally consistent with behavioral outcomes, though non-linearly related (§4.3–4.4). A naive strict-match scorer masked this picture, producing a false capability collapse (0.550 vs 0.969 under canonicalized scoring) that artifact-aware evaluation and pre-registered TOST equivalence testing — to our knowledge unprecedented in the DPO literature as the primary evaluation framework for capability assessment — prevented.

Our contributions are:

1. A graded, task-selective alignment tax driven by rejected-collapse dynamics and style-axis aggregate pressure, with non-monotonic format-break dynamics at margin ≈ 3 and a falsified pre-registered prediction on held-out per-pair margin (§4.1–4.3).
2. Task-selective gold-logp deflation with selective protection demonstrated on extraction (8/8 seeds, paired statistic), robust across quantization backends (§4.4).
3. An artifact-aware evaluation protocol with pre-registered TOST equivalence testing, whose falsification chronology (three falsified hypotheses, two refined characterizations) documents how pre-registration forced successive refinement of initially simpler claims (§3, §5.2).

This study operates within explicit scope conditions: LoRA (not full fine-tuning), a single 4B model, length-normalized rewards with an SFT retention term (weight 0.5), easily-separable synthetic preferences, and three structured exact-format tasks. We do not study safety or refusal tasks — where Razin et al. demonstrated catastrophic displacement — full fine-tuning, naturalistic human-written preferences, or models above 4B parameters. These are stated boundaries, not implicit weaknesses.
