# §5 Discussion

## 5.1 A Two-Level Mechanistic Account

The behavioral and probability-layer findings form a coherent — though not causally proven — account of how length-normalized DPO affects downstream capability.

At the training level, the SFT retention term (weight 0.5) anchors chosen-completion likelihood, channeling DPO's optimization pressure entirely into rejected suppression. Rejected reward falls to −14.2 while chosen stays within ±0.05 (§4.3). The style axis contributes 65% more aggregate suppression than the correctness axis, because it has 2.2× more pairs — not because each style pair is more potent (the held-out data confirms per-pair tool\_calling margin is higher; §4.3, Table 5). This aggregate pressure is consistent with why the task most format-orthogonal to the verbose style axis (router) absorbs the most damage, while the task with direct tool\_calling coverage (json) absorbs less.

At the probability level, gold-logp deflation is task-selective and directionally consistent with the behavioral gradient: extraction deflates least (−0.15 nats/token), json intermediate (−1.04), router most (−1.47). On extraction — the best-preserved task, and the one whose gold completions overlap most with training-pair chosen completions (§3.3) — gold is selectively protected relative to incorrect controls (8/8 seeds, paired stat +0.21 to +0.30). This is consistent with Ren and Sutherland's squeezing effect: negative gradients on rejected completions concentrate probability mass onto the highest-probability token at each position, which for extraction items — whose structured outputs share entity vocabulary with training-pair completions (§3.3) — may be the gold completion. Gold declines some, but less than everything else. Router items, whose terse enum format is maximally dissimilar from the verbose training pairs, may not benefit from the same concentration effect — consistent with their substantially larger gold deflation (−1.47 vs −0.15 nats/token).

However, the two levels do not form a single causal chain. Gold-logp magnitude is non-linearly related to behavioral outcomes: router's gold deflates only 1.4× more than json's, but its behavioral degradation is 8.6× worse. The behavioral gradient is better explained by format-alignment to the style-axis aggregate pressure than by gold-logp dynamics. We present the two levels as complementary observations — rejected collapse explains which tasks are pressured; gold-logp dynamics describe how probability mass redistributes — without claiming one causes the other.

## 5.2 Falsification Chronology

The pre-registered analysis plan forced three falsifications and two refinements over the course of this study, each producing a more precise characterization than the hypothesis it replaced. Three additional hypotheses were abandoned during the design phase before the confirmatory matrix was run (Appendix C): a causal-clamp experiment was dropped as confounded by KL and total optimization, router was dropped as a primary endpoint after pilot trajectory analysis revealed a non-monotonic transient, and targeted displacement was dropped after control probes pointed to global drift rather than targeted gold deflation.

Three hypotheses were falsified outright. First, the bounded-null headline — that all three tasks would be preserved within ±5 points — was rejected when the pre-registered falsifier fired on both json and router (§4.1). Second, an apparent extraction capability collapse at margin ≈ 3 (accuracy 0.550) was traced to a scorer artifact: under canonicalized scoring, the same checkpoint scores 0.969, with 93% of apparent errors being surface-form register shifts. The measurement was killed, not refined. Third, the pre-registered prediction that style-axis held-out margin would exceed tool\_calling margin was falsified at every checkpoint on every seed (§4.3, Table 5), revealing that style dominance is an aggregate-volume effect, not a per-pair potency advantage.

Two characterizations were refined. The pilot data's "margin-independent early drop" pattern — where router degraded early then stayed flat regardless of margin — was replaced by a more precise description: a damage window at margin ≈ 1–3 with two distinct processes (transient format-break + monotonic content-error), followed by stabilization (§4.2). And the initial "gold barely moves" characterization (from the nf4 matrix, |Δgold| ≤ 0.01) was refined to "gold is selectively protected but task-selectively deflates" when fp16 same-backend measurement revealed substantial json and router gold deflation (§4.4).

Each pre-registered decision gate forced a more precise characterization of what actually happened. The result is more nuanced than any single prior framing predicted — and more informative than either a clean null or a uniform degradation finding would have been.

## 5.3 Engagement with Prior Work

Our data engages three specific claims from the displacement literature, with the caveat that our training objective includes an SFT retention term (weight 0.5) on chosen completions — making it DPO + retention, not vanilla DPO.

Rafailov et al. predict from their Q-function framework that chosen log-prob should decrease under DPO from an SFT-initialized model. Our training objective includes an SFT retention term on chosen completions, which directly counteracts this decline. The chosen-flatness we observe (±0.05 over 7 units of margin growth) is consistent with this design choice rather than a contradiction of their framework. The chosen-flatness reported in §4.3 is therefore at least partly a consequence of the training objective, not purely an empirical finding about DPO dynamics; it confirms the retention term is effective in this regime but cannot speak to whether chosen would decline under standard DPO without retention.

Cal-DPO adds a calibration term to prevent chosen-reward decline. Our regime includes an SFT retention term that achieves a similar anchoring effect by a different mechanism. The finding that chosen is flat in our data does not demonstrate Cal-DPO's concern is unfounded — it demonstrates the concern is already addressed by the retention term. The more informative observation is that even with chosen explicitly anchored, the behavioral alignment tax remains: router degrades −30 points and json shows one-directional loss, driven entirely by the rejected-suppression channel that the retention term does not protect.

IPO was explicitly designed to bound the implicit reward margin, treating unbounded growth as the core pathology. Our data shows margin growing to ~7 (β-scaled) with graded behavioral effects — the unbounded growth that IPO constrains produces task-selective damage, not the uniform catastrophe the bounding was designed to prevent. This does not invalidate IPO generally; it characterizes a regime where the damage is concentrated in the margin 1–3 window and is format-selective rather than universal.

## 5.4 Format-Alignment: What It Explains and What It Does Not

The behavioral gradient (extraction preserved → json small loss → router catastrophic) is directionally consistent with format-alignment to the preference data's style axis across all 8 seeds. But with only three tasks, we report this co-occurrence without claiming a causal mechanism.

Two specific limits deserve attention. First, extraction has zero direct preference coverage yet is preserved — so coverage absence alone does not predict degradation. The distinguishing factor may be format-orthogonality (extraction's structured JSON is neither aligned with nor antagonistic to the verbose style), domain overlap (§3.3, same entities appear in training), or the retention term anchoring chosen completions that share extraction's entity vocabulary. All three co-vary with extraction's preservation and cannot be disentangled with three tasks. Second, json has 200 on-target tool\_calling pairs yet still degrades slightly. The per-axis decomposition explains this: tool\_calling coverage protects against the correctness axis but not against the dominant style-axis aggregate pressure. The damage mechanism (style-rejected suppression) and the protection mechanism (tool\_calling coverage) operate on different axes of the preference data.

Json's substantial gold deflation (−1.04 nats/token) paired with only minor behavioral loss (−3.5 points) suggests that structured output formats with redundant constraints — typed fields, function signatures, required keys — may buffer greedy-decode behavior against large probability-mass redistribution. Extraction is also structured JSON yet deflates far less (−0.15), so format redundancy alone does not explain the difference; format-alignment to the preference data co-varies. With two structured tasks and two co-varying variables, this hypothesis is illustrative, not testable.

## 5.5 Practical Implications

For practitioners running length-normalized DPO on small models with structured-output tasks: (1) monitor format-break rate at margin ≈ 3, not just endpoint accuracy — the transient spike is where damage concentrates, and it may not be visible at the final checkpoint; (2) audit preference-data composition for style-axis aggregate dominance, as the behavioral gradient tracks the aggregate training gradient (total margin per axis), not margin magnitude or per-pair generalization strength; (3) include an SFT retention term on chosen completions — our regime's chosen-flatness is at least partly attributable to this design choice, and without it, chosen-reward decline may exacerbate the alignment tax.

## 5.6 Limitations

This study operates within explicit scope conditions: LoRA (not full fine-tuning), a single 4B model, length-normalized rewards, easily-separable synthetic preferences, and three structured exact-format tasks. We do not study safety or refusal tasks, where Razin et al. demonstrated catastrophic displacement. The preference pairs use synthetic degradation with clean chosen-rejected separation; under naturalistic human preferences with higher chosen-rejected similarity (high CHES scores), the displacement dynamics Razin et al. characterize may produce different outcomes. Results may not generalize beyond these conditions.

Three pre-registered secondary analyses were affected by implementation gaps. Control-relative gold-logp (the paired GOLD\_PROTECTED statistic) could be computed only for extraction, because controls A and B require task-specific design; json and router gold deflation is reported as absolute Δgold only. Chosen/rejected reward decomposition was computed post-hoc via a dedicated GPU inference run on saved adapters; per-category held-out margin was computed via Mac inference on saved adapters. KL(policy || reference) was not logged during training and was never computed in the training loop; it was computed post-hoc from saved adapters on extraction gold completions only — drift on other tasks may be larger. A preliminary probe on two seeds reported KL = 0.072 and 0.013 nats/token under nf4, suggesting mild drift, but matrix-wide KL was not computed during training. We report these as implementation gaps, not post-hoc exclusions; the primary TOST analysis was unaffected.

The format-alignment mechanism rests on n = 3 tasks — insufficient to distinguish format-orthogonality from coverage, domain overlap, or task difficulty as the causal factor. The checkpoint schedule (margin1, margin3, margin5, final) cannot pinpoint the damage window more precisely than "between margin ~1 and ~3." Gold-logp absolute magnitudes differ substantially between fp16 and nf4 backends; the task-selective ordering is robust across both, but absolute values are not directly comparable (§4.4 and Appendix G).

A complete reconciliation of all pre-registered secondaries against their status (reported, filled post-hoc, or implementation gap) is provided in Appendix C.
