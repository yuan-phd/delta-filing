# §2 Related Work

**Likelihood displacement.** Razin et al. (ICLR 2025) identify likelihood displacement under DPO: chosen log-prob decreases while probability mass may flow to out-of-distribution or harmful completions. They introduce the CHES score (centered hidden embedding similarity) to identify preference pairs whose embedding proximity drives catastrophic displacement. Their own analysis notes displacement is "not necessarily problematic" — catastrophic only when mass flows to opposite-meaning responses. Our study operates in a regime with an SFT retention term that anchors chosen likelihood, so the chosen-decline dynamic their framework describes does not manifest (§4.3). Whether displacement would emerge without the retention term in our setup is untested.

**The squeezing effect.** Ren and Sutherland (ICLR 2025) show that DPO's gradient is dominated by negative gradients on rejected completions, concentrating probability mass onto the highest-probability token at each position — the squeezing effect. Their analysis demonstrates the effect at the single-token level on Pythia and Qwen; we observe dynamics consistent with the same mechanism on multi-token structured completions, though the transfer across settings is assumed rather than proven. Our reward decomposition (§4.3) is textbook squeezing: rejected reward falls to −14.2 while chosen stays within ±0.05 (with the retention term contributing to chosen stability). The squeezing framework also provides a candidate explanation for task-selective gold deflation (§4.4): extraction items, whose outputs involve entities also present in training data, may benefit from mass concentration onto gold tokens; router items, whose terse format is maximally dissimilar from the verbose training pairs, may not.

**Ordering preservation and calibration.** Cal-DPO (Xiao et al., NeurIPS 2024) observes that DPO preserves preference ordering while absolute likelihoods deflate, and proposes calibrating the implicit reward to match ground-truth reward scale, preventing chosen-reward decline. Our regime includes an SFT retention term that achieves a similar anchoring effect on chosen completions (§3.1). The finding that chosen is flat does not demonstrate Cal-DPO's concern is unfounded — it demonstrates the concern is already addressed in our training objective. The more informative observation is that even with chosen explicitly anchored, the behavioral alignment tax remains on off-target tasks (§4.1), driven entirely by rejected suppression (§4.3).

**Bounding the margin.** IPO (Azar et al., AISTATS 2024) replaces DPO's log-sigmoid with a squared loss to bound the implicit reward margin, treating unbounded growth as the core pathology. Our data shows margin growing to ~7 (β-scaled) with graded, format-selective behavioral effects rather than the uniform catastrophe the bounding was designed to prevent (§4.1, §4.2). This does not invalidate IPO generally; it characterizes a regime where the damage is concentrated in a margin 1–3 window and is task-selective.

**Chosen-reward predictions.** Rafailov et al. (COLM 2024) predict from a Q-function framework that chosen log-prob should decrease under DPO. Our training objective includes an SFT retention term that directly counteracts this; we discuss the implications for interpreting our chosen-flatness result in §5.3.

**Equivalence testing and scoring methodology.** Manzoni (arXiv 2603.24044, 2026 preprint) uses TOST equivalence testing with 8 seeds for SFT capability preservation in MoE fine-tuning; a concurrent paper on task-oriented dialogue (Bao and Abisado, "Pareto-Aware Dual-Preference Optimization," Symmetry 2026) applies TOST as incidental validation in a DPO context. To our knowledge, pre-registered TOST as the primary evaluation framework for DPO capability assessment is unprecedented in the DPO literature, though TOST is well-established in bioequivalence and related fields. On scoring, Baxi (arXiv 2512.17920, 2025 preprint) separates constraint compliance from semantic accuracy in LLM evaluation, establishing the conceptual decomposition; our contribution is the trajectory dynamics (transient format-break spike versus monotonic content-error rise, §4.2). Minerva (Lewkowycz et al., NeurIPS 2022) provides precedent for answer-normalization preventing false negatives in exact-match evaluation; our scorer artifact (0.550 → 0.969 under canonicalization) is a concrete alignment-specific instance.

**DPO dynamics and alignment-tax context.** Li (arXiv 2603.19335, 2026 preprint) presents OXRL, evaluating 20 DPO variants under Bonferroni correction and finding none outperforms vanilla DPO, consistent with their finding that loss-function modifications produce minimal behavioral differences at matched compute. On the alignment tax more broadly, Young (arXiv 2603.00047, 2026 preprint) frames the alignment tax geometrically as tracking the projection of the safety direction onto the capability subspace — our format-axis-alignment finding (§4.1) is an empirical instantiation. Pollanen (arXiv 2601.17260, 2026 preprint) reports that margin can anticorrelate with reasoning capability (r = −0.91 for LLaMA on logic tasks when sweeping β across a dense grid); their study varies β rather than observing within-run margin growth, making the result related but distinct from our trajectory analysis.
