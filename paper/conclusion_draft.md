# Conclusion

The alignment tax under length-normalized DPO with an SFT retention term is real but graded and task-selective, with router's catastrophic loss temporally structured — concentrated in a margin 1–3 damage window where transient format-break and persistent content-error operate as distinct processes. The retention term anchors chosen completions while DPO's gradient pressure channels entirely into rejected-completion suppression, producing a behavioral gradient that tracks format-alignment to the preference data's style axis rather than margin magnitude. Artifact-aware scoring corrected a false-collapse signal, and pre-registered TOST equivalence testing prevented both false-positive and false-negative capability claims; a disclosed chronology of three falsified hypotheses and two refined characterizations documents how pre-registration forced successive refinement of initially simpler claims. The finding that margin growth to ~7 β-scaled units produces graded rather than uniform degradation offers a differentiated picture — preservation where format-alignment is maintained, substantial risk where it is not — and a methodological template for future alignment-tax studies. These findings are established under specific scope conditions — LoRA, a single 4B model, length-normalized rewards with an SFT retention term, and easily-separable synthetic preferences — and may not transfer to full fine-tuning, naturalistic preferences, or safety-critical tasks.
